# Futures basis tops a short, sober add-list

Almost nothing Zobiris doesn't already track has proven, tested power to call Bitcoin's *direction* days to weeks ahead. The useful additions are mostly **risk and regime flags**. They tell you when the ground is fragile, not which way price goes next. The best-supported new input is the **futures basis (carry)**: the annualized premium of futures over spot. A Bank for International Settlements (BIS) working paper found that **high carry predicts crashes**. Next come options-implied volatility (DVOL) against realized volatility, a cross-asset stress pack (real yields, yen, credit spreads, bond volatility, and BTC's rolling correlation with Nasdaq), and a supply-event and token-unlock calendar for altcoins. On-chain valuation gauges such as MVRV and realized price are **cycle context only**. Sentiment gauges mostly echo price. The popular "global M2 leads Bitcoin by 10–12 weeks" claim looks **curve-fit**. As of late October 2026 the market reads calm and deleveraged: BTC is near $81k, DVOL is at its lowest since 2021, funding is neutral, open interest is down about 31% in a month, and US spot demand is weak (the Coinbase premium has been negative for weeks). That is a compressed-volatility setup, and the evidence says nothing about which way it breaks. One caveat covers every reading below: the venue APIs were blocked during research, so most current numbers come from dated secondary reports and need a live re-pull before they go into any gate.

## Basis is the one derivatives gauge with a published multi-week claim

BIS Working Paper 1087 found that BTC/ETH futures carry averages **above 10% a year, peaks near 60%**, and that "a high crypto carry predicts future price crashes". The driver is retail demand for leverage, not interest rates ([BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm)). The October 2025 cascade showed the mechanism. Basis compression forced carry trades to unwind, about **$19.1B was liquidated**, and BTC fell 14% over 10–11 October ([Amberdata](https://blog.amberdata.io/the-carry-trade-that-broke-how-basis-compression-triggered-the-cascade); [Coin360](https://coin360.com/news/crypto-market-liquidation-2025-trump-tariffs)). The paper is unreviewed and its effect size wasn't visible, so it earns a flag, not a trigger. In August 2026 the futures yield had dropped from above 20% to **below Treasury yields** ([FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/cryptocurrencies/news/the-bitcoin-futures-yield-collapse-once-over-20-now-less-than-treasury-notes-202608031149)). A low basis means the crash flag is currently quiet. No current CME figure was found.

**DVOL versus realized volatility** is the next most useful gauge. DVOL priced more volatility than actually arrived on **73.7% of days** from 2022 to 2026. It closed at **37.33 on 19 Oct**, the lowest since March 2021 ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/bitcoin-options-implied-volatility-hits-92-lows-since-2021)). It conditions trades rather than pointing them: it sets expected move size, stop distances and position sizes. Put skew is rising, with one-month 25-delta skew moving to −2.19 ([BIT](https://www.bit.com/research/bitcoin-put-skew-leverage-divergence-2026-10-12)). The "skew flips before crashes" story rests on **two events** ([K33](https://k33.com/research/archive/articles/medium-term-far-out-the-money-option-flows-foreshadowing-bitcoin-sell-offs)).

The **Coinbase premium** (US spot price minus offshore price) is cheap to compute and adds a US-demand view alongside ETF flows. It was negative for **20 straight days to 13 Oct (−0.14%)** ([TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/investing/30900)). No one has published a test of what follows such streaks. The Kimchi (Korea) premium is ordinary at about 1%. Funding extremes add little beyond Zobiris's Kraken funding feed: the one recent paper finds about ±1.8% moves over 24 hours, and the direction depends on the regime ([SSRN 7520320](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7520320)). That fits your own null result on alt rebounds. **Max pain, CME gaps, long/short ratios and liquidation "magnets" have no rigorous test at all**. The well-known "95% of CME gaps fill" figure traces back to a TradingView post ([TradingView](https://tr.tradingview.com/chart/BTC1%21/xKBEbmYq-BTC-CME-Gap-Playout)). Use your Hyperliquid liquidation map to see where forced selling would speed up a move, not as a price target.

|---|---|---|---|

## On-chain gauges describe the season, not the week

No on-chain metric has replicated out-of-sample evidence for BTC direction at a days-to-weeks horizon. MVRV-Z, NUPL and CVDD rules beat buy-and-hold over 2013–2025, but each made **only three trades**, with thresholds chosen after the fact ([Grobys et al.](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/6575b90c-6140-44e7-a798-011a1793d134/content)). Hash ribbons did **21% worse than plain dollar-cost averaging** ([Amdax](https://medium.com/amdax-asset-management/the-bitcoin-hash-rate-a-failing-investment-tool-e343cd99719c)), and hashrate follows price rather than leading it ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1911-8074/13/11/263)). The most honest holdout test (BGeometrics, Mar 2024–Oct 2026) found the best on-chain rules only modestly beat a plain **50-day moving-average filter** (Sharpe 0.98 vs 0.62). An exchange-reserve rule with a training Sharpe of 2.07 went flat out of sample ([BGeometrics](https://bgeometrics.com/blog/2026-10-bg-lab-onchain-rules-tested-honestly/)). Exchange flows carry real information, but at **1–6 hour** horizons. Their clearest value is for volatility, and stablecoin inflows to exchanges are the piece that predicts BTC returns ([arXiv 2411.06327](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.06327)).

Current readings: **MVRV 1.50 at $81,257 on 22 Oct**, from the free Coin Metrics community API ([Coin Metrics](https://community-api.coinmetrics.io/v4/timeseries/asset-metrics?assets=btc&metrics=PriceUSD,CapMVRVCur,HashRate&frequency=1d&start_time=2026-10-14&page_size=100)). That puts the cycle in mid-range, with no buy or sell extreme. Glassnode puts the **spot-ETF cost basis near $81.5K**, which price has bounced off twice. It puts the True Market Mean near $78K and two-year buyers' cost basis above $88K ([Glassnode](https://research.glassnode.com/the-week-onchain-week-41-2026/)). These levels matter because traders watch them, not because they predict anything. Exchange-flow data needs paid CryptoQuant or Glassnode tiers, and providers disagree on reserve levels ([NodeWitness](https://nodewitness.com/en/analysis/cycle-and-valuation/)).

## Macro gauges work as stress filters, and the M2 lead is curve-fit

Zobiris already tracks Fed, CPI and jobs data. What's missing is the *market's* stress plumbing. The August 2024 yen carry unwind knocked BTC down by **up to 20%** ([BIS Bulletin 90](https://www.bis.org/publications/bulletin-90-market-turbulence-and-carry-trade-unwind-august-2024.pdf)), so a fast yen rally is a tail-risk trigger. USDJPY was 156.5 on 22 Oct. BTC's correlation with Nasdaq is **strongly regime-dependent**: its 30-day value swung from −0.68 to +0.74 within a month in early 2026 ([Cryptonews.net](https://cryptonews.net/news/bitcoin/33382637/)), and Grayscale put the 90-day value at 33% in September ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/crypto/cryptocurrency/26/09/61593730/bitcoin-gold-nasdaq-composite-correlation-debasement-trade-hedge)). You already have the Nasdaq feed, so computing the rolling correlation costs almost nothing. It tells you whether macro or crypto-specific flows are driving the market. Real yields, high-yield credit spreads and bond volatility (MOVE) fit as risk-off filters. No study shows they *lead* BTC.

The global-M2 "10–12 week lead" falls apart on inspection. Analysts' chosen lags range from about **10.7 to 15.4 weeks**, small shifts re-fit the tops, and most of the series' daily movement is really the dollar ([TradingView/NewsBTC](https://www.tradingview.com/news/newsbtc:9d7b56c3d094b:0-global-m2-can-t-predict-bitcoin-price-says-quant-analyst/); [TechToken](https://techtoken.in/why-global-m2-may-be-misleading-bitcoin-forecasts/)). Stablecoin issuance tends to **follow** BTC rather than lead it ([Wei 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165176518302556)). It works as a demand-state check: supply is **$301B, down from a $322B peak** ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/stablecoins)). FOMC meets 27–28 Oct.

## Sentiment mostly echoes price, while supply events hit before the date

The best sentiment study, covering eight years of the Fear & Greed index, found it **does not beat the historical average out of sample**. It leans toward momentum, not contrarian ([Digital Finance 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42521-026-00218-y)). It read **50 (Neutral) on 23 Oct** ([alternative.me](https://api.alternative.me/fng/?limit=14)). LLM-scored news gives no out-of-sample gain ([Journal of Big Data](https://www.springerprofessional.de/news-sentiment-analysis-using-chatgpt-for-bitcoin-price-dynamics/52359016)). Whale deposits explain under 6% of later moves ([Presto](https://www.prestolabs.io/research/whale-alerts-are-they-tradable)). The one sturdy peer-reviewed weekly result is **momentum** (+3.16% next week per standard deviation of this week's return) and attention ([Liu & Tsyvinski, NBER](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf)). Your Kraken price feed already captures that, which makes plain momentum the right **benchmark** every new indicator must beat.

For altcoins, **token unlocks** are the clearest event effect. They put negative pressure on price in 90% of 16,000 events, and the pressure starts about **30 days before** the unlock ([Keyrock](https://keyrock.com/from-locked-to-liquidity-what-16000-token-unlocks-teach-us/)). Large unlocks average about −15% over ±30 days, then partly rebound ([Coinmonks](https://medium.com/coinmonks/i-backtested-shorting-token-unlocks-heres-why-i-m-not-trading-it-yet-42e237d40d9a)). That fits naturally as a demotion input on alt-flush candidates. For BTC, big known sales have been "sell the rumour": the fear comes with the first wallet moves, and the actual sales get absorbed ([NYDIG](https://www.nydig.com/research/estimating-the-price-impact-from-large-sellers)). Dated overhangs to calendar:

- **MSCI exclusion of Strategy-type companies, 30 Nov** ([Mempool Brief](https://mempoolbrief.com/articles/strategy-holds-848-517-btc-after-a-year-of-selling-buybacks-and-redefined-metric-b9184425))
- **Nakamoto's Kraken loan, December** ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/07/24/bitcoin-treasury-companies-sell-up-repay-debt-pivot-to-ai-as-share-prices-collapse))
- **MARA's ongoing exchange deposits** ([news.bitcoin.com](https://news.bitcoin.com/mining/mara-bitcoin-1000-btc-exchanges-shares-sink/))
- **Mt. Gox's ~34k BTC**, now deferred to Oct 2027 ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/381392/mt-gox-pushes-repayment-deadline-to-2027-with-2-8b-in-bitcoin-still-to-distribute))

## Conclusion

The research mostly narrows the job. Zobiris already holds the one input with a peer-reviewed weekly edge, price momentum. The cheapest wins come from flags that tell the existing gates when to shrink or stand down, not from new buy triggers. That fits the project's rule that research can demote but never authorize. Each test should be pre-registered with frozen thresholds and costs, and judged against a 50-day momentum baseline and a volatility-matched null. That standard, not the published claims, decides what earns weight.

Right now DVOL is at multi-year lows, leverage has been flushed and US demand is soft. Basis and DVOL are the two gauges built to spot the moment that calm ends. Wiring them up before the 30 Nov MSCI date would be well-timed.
