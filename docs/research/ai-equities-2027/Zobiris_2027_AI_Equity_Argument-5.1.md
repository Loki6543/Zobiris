# ZOBIRIS / 2027
## Investing in the AI Boom Without Depending on Calling the Top
*A growth-first equity thesis, response to the evidence review, and portfolio risk framework*

**Version 5.1.** Revised 29 October 2026 from version 5. Evidence cutoff: sources checked through 29 October; each observation keeps its own reporting date. No live entry price is assessed.

**What changed in v5.1.**
- The owner set a loss budget: at most 24% of the portfolio.
- Sizing is reworked to fit it (§5, §6), and 80/10/10 becomes a ceiling, not a target.
- Alphabet's financing is checked against its SEC filing (§3).
- The EPS interpretation is softened (§2, §10).
- A capex × monetization grid is added (§1).
- The exit test is clarified by a registered amendment (§11).

v5 had already closed four items: the trend test was preregistered, EPS was calendarised, yields were added, and Alphabet's trailing-year free cash flow was verified.

**Mandate.** Pursue the potential 2027 opportunity mainly through stocks, with a smaller crypto allocation. Control concentration and limit the damage from a failed thesis or a late exit, while keeping substantial upside participation.

**Central argument.** AI investment and adoption can sustain growth in selected suppliers and platforms. Shareholders benefit only when sustainable earnings and cash flow per share justify the entry valuation. The opportunity is conditional. It does not require a capital-preservation portfolio, or a prediction that every member of the Magnificent Seven recovers.

**Portfolio proposal (v5.1).** The owner's loss budget is a maximum of 24%. Sized to that, the research allocation is about **51% invested and 49% cash**, keeping v5's sleeve proportions (§5).

The earlier 80/10/10 mix is now a ceiling that applies only if the loss budget is raised. In the document's worst stress, 80/10/10 loses 42%.

This is not an optimized portfolio or an approved order list. A stress test is not a guarantee: a worse year than any scenario here could exceed 24%.

**Document authority.** This document supersedes earlier thesis text and allocation proposals. It does not change holdings, orders, deployed Zobiris gates or model parameters. The exit rules below are test specifications, not validated safeguards.

## 1 / The investment proposition and its limits
The proposed opportunity is an earnings-led continuation of the AI investment cycle into 2027.
- **Platforms:** established platforms have the distribution, customers and infrastructure to monetize adoption.
- **Suppliers:** suppliers can benefit from spending across competing platforms.

These are plausible economic mechanisms, not proof of an attractive stock price.

**Productivity evidence.** Micro-level evidence supports the possibility of real value creation.
- The published customer-support study reports an average productivity improvement of about 15% in its setting [11].
- The productivity J-curve explains why complementary investment can delay measured gains [12].

Neither result identifies which security captures the gains, or makes calendar 2027 the payoff year.

**How gains can leak away.**
- Competition can pass benefits on to customers.
- Infrastructure costs can consume operating gains.
- Equity issuance can spread gains across more shares.
- Supplier revenues and customer capex are connected transactions, not independent confirmations of profitable final demand.

| Bull mechanism | Evidence needed | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Paid AI adoption | Recurring revenue, utilization, profitable workloads | Adoption grows but customers won't pay enough |
| Operating leverage | Margins and normalized cash per share improve | Depreciation, energy and inference costs absorb gains |
| Valuation reset | Lower price relative to unchanged sustainable earnings | Price falls alongside larger earnings revisions |
| Funding capacity | Affordable obligations, manageable dilution | Credit support and financing burden shareholders |

**Capex × monetization (v5.1).** The 60% AI exposure is not one bet. Spending direction and payback can diverge, and each combination favours a different sleeve:

| | Strong monetization | Weak monetization |
|---|---|---|
| **Capex rising** | Good for both platforms and suppliers | Suppliers gain; platform cash flow suffers |
| **Capex falling** | Platform FCF improves; supplier growth weakens | Bad for both |

Evidence on monetization is the thinnest part of this document. Before any platform overweight, it needs, per company:
- AI revenue, or a defensible proxy;
- workload economics;
- depreciation assumptions;
- incremental operating profit;
- FCF per fully diluted share against cumulative investment.

Before any supplier overweight, it needs:
- backlog quality and cancellations;
- customer concentration;
- inventory and pricing;
- mid-cycle margins;
- customer-financing exposure.

Falsifying evidence should be written down before results are seen.

**Investment horizon.** Evaluate calendar 2027 separately from purchases made in late 2026. If both return windows matter, record both, and don't pick the better one afterwards. A positive annual return can coexist with a severe drawdown during the year.

**What is not established.** This work does not show that all seven stocks will rise, beat cash or Bitcoin, regain their highs, or avoid a crash before 2028. A price below a past high is not a margin of safety.

## 5 / Growth-first portfolio construction

**Sizing rule (v5.1).** The owner's loss budget is 24% at most. The document's worst preregistered stress, the AI-centered bust, must not exceed it. Applied to v5's sleeve proportions, that gives:

| Sleeve | v5.1 ceiling | v5 (only if the budget is raised) | Role and constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI platforms / monetizers | 23% | 40% | Main earnings thesis. Candidate pool: MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META. Eligibility and price still matter. |
| Semiconductors / AI infrastructure | 11% | 20% | Supplier participation. NVDA, AVGO and TSM are candidates, not three independent hedges. |
| Other equities | 11% | 20% | Different earnings drivers. A look-through cap applies (below). |
| Crypto | 6% | 10% | Needs its own justification and loss share (below). The BTC gate remains separate. |
| Cash / short bills | 49% | 10% | Default home for anything not yet qualified. |
| **Total** | **100%** | **100%** | No leverage. |

**What this fixes.**
- The 23% platform ceiling no longer forces all four names to exactly 10%. Selective weighting of about 6% each, or fewer names, is now possible.
- Sleeve figures are ceilings. Until valuations (§9) and monetization evidence (§1) qualify a name, its share stays in cash. So the effective position today is mostly cash, which applies the document's own argument rather than calling a top.

**An alternative mix, for comparison.** Re-mixing toward platforms and away from suppliers and crypto (28% / 8% / 12% / 4% / 48% cash) gives a similar stress profile: −23.2% in the AI bust and +14.5% in a boom. The choice between this and the proportional mix should follow the valuation work, not precede it.

**Caps.**
- 10% maximum in any single company held directly; 12% total per issuer including funds.
- **Look-through thematic cap (v5.1):** total AI-linked exposure must not exceed 40% of the portfolio, counted through funds. That includes hyperscalers, semiconductors, AI infrastructure, and AI-dependent power and data-centre names. It applies to the "other equities" sleeve too, so a broad index there counts its AI holdings.

**Rebalancing bands (v5.1).**
- Rebalance a sleeve when it drifts more than 5 percentage points from its weight.
- Breaches caused by new purchases are not allowed.
- A breach caused by market gains is trimmed at the next monthly review, not immediately.

**Crypto's share of risk.** In the AI-centered bust, crypto supplies about 17% of the loss from 6% of capital. That needs its own justification, and the BTC gate evidence (Deflated Sharpe 0.33, 57% overfitting probability) is too weak to supply it.

**Scope.** These weights apply to investable risk capital. They exclude an emergency reserve and known near-term spending. Current holdings are not a constraint: the owner treats them as changeable.

Even at these ceilings, the AI-linked sleeves and crypto can fall together. That shared exposure is what the 24% budget is sized against.

## 6 / Stress the portfolio, including being late
These are deterministic one-period total-return scenarios at the proposed fixed weights. They assume no rebalancing and exclude taxes, trading costs and slippage. They are not forecasts or probabilities.

| Scenario | Platforms 40% | Suppliers 20% | Other 20% | Crypto 10% | Cash 10% | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Boom extends | +25% | +40% | +12% | +35% | +3% | +24.2% |
| Mixed year | +8% | −10% | +5% | −20% | +3% | +0.5% |
| Rate / valuation shock | −25% | −35% | −15% | −45% | +3% | −24.2% |
| Severe broad decline | −40% | −40% | −40% | −65% | 0% | −38.5% |
| AI-centered bust | −45% | −60% | −25% | −70% | 0% | −42.0% |

**The same scenarios at the v5.1 ceilings (23 / 11 / 11 / 6 / 49):**

| Scenario | v5.1 ceilings | v5 80/10/10 | Within the 24% budget? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boom extends | +15.0% | +24.2% | — |
| Mixed year | +1.6% | +0.5% | — |
| Rate / valuation shock | −12.5% | −24.2% | Yes |
| Severe broad decline | −21.9% | −38.5% | Yes |
| AI-centered bust | −23.9% | −42.0% | Yes (the binding case) |
| Harsher stress (platforms −60%, suppliers −75%, other −40%, crypto −80%) | −31.3% | −55.0% | **No** |

**Cash convention.** Cash earns +3% in the three milder scenarios and 0% in the two crash scenarios. This is a conservative way to allow for rates falling in a crisis. At 3.9%, each cash point adds 0.039 points.

**The harsher stress exceeds the budget.** Fitting it as well would need about 61% cash: roughly 17 / 9 / 9 / 4, with the rest in cash. That is the price of covering the critique's worst case. A worse year than any scenario here remains possible.

**Slow deterioration (v5.1).** A one-year crash is not the only way to fail. Suppose platforms and suppliers lose 12% a year for two years, with a trend rule that never triggers cleanly. At the v5.1 ceilings, with other sleeves flat, that costs about 7.7% over two years (34% × 22.6%), before cash interest. That is inside the budget, but it is the case a trend rule handles worst.

**A good year can still end badly.** A 30% gain followed by a 40% loss leaves 1.30 × 0.60 = 0.78 of the starting value. Recovering from a 38.5% loss needs a 62.6% gain.

**Late exit.** Suppose the AI-centered bust has already cut the portfolio from 100 to 58. Half of each remaining risky holding is then moved to cash, and the remaining risky assets fall another 20%. That costs about 4.8 points more, ending near 53.2.

**Opportunity cost.** Cutting too early can miss a rebound, trigger taxes and cause repeated whipsaws.

The final invested amount must be one you could lose 40% of, without relying on an untested exit to save it.

## 7 / Participation rules and an exit process to test
This is research policy, not a live trading system. No stop, order or deployed Zobiris threshold is created.

| State | Proposed evidence / action | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Normal participation | Qualified fundamentals; caps respected. Earnings reviewed quarterly, exposure monthly. | A strong business can still fall sharply. |
| Risk review | A 10% portfolio drawdown from a flow-adjusted high triggers a review and freezes discretionary increases. | 10% is a review level, not a predicted crash boundary. |
| Trend candidate | Cut half of an equity sleeve after two completed weekly closes below its 40-week SMA. Execute at the next session. **Preregistered v5 (§11).** | It lags; sudden crashes and sideways markets can defeat it. |
| Fundamental deterioration | Assess demand, margins, financing and normalized cash per share. | Judgment is not a backtested signal. |
| Re-entry candidate | Restore exposure after two completed weekly closes above the same SMA. | It can buy back higher. Missed rebound and costs must be counted. |

**Crypto stays separate.** The existing monthly BTC gate remains unless its owner deliberately approves a change.

No leverage, no automatic averaging down, and no increase in risk budget because losses make the thesis feel more urgent.

## 8 / Zobiris evidence and macro interpretation
**The BTC gate replay.** The Zobiris replay reports that a monthly BTC gate reduced maximum drawdown from 37% to 30%. Macro tilts added no measurable advantage. The best Deflated Sharpe Ratio was 0.33, with a 57% probability of overfitting. These are reported results, not reproduced here.

**Crash base rates.** Greenwood, Shleifer and You find that sharp industry run-ups can precede elevated crash risk without reliably forecasting low average returns. Their 53% crash frequency applies to a particular historical run-up definition over a two-year horizon. It is not a 2027 probability for these seven stocks [10].

**Rates.** On 28 October the Fed held the target range at 3.75–4.00%. Two dissenters preferred a hike [9].

## 9 / Valuation and validation before implementation
For a stock with positive, consistently defined earnings:

**Price return = (1 + EPS growth) × (1 + P/E change) − 1**

Add dividends separately for total return. If forward P/E is used at both year-end dates, the earnings horizon rolls forward.

| EPS growth | P/E −25% | P/E −15% | P/E unchanged | P/E +10% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| +10% | −17.5% | −6.5% | +10.0% | +21.0% |
| +20% | −10.0% | +2.0% | +20.0% | +32.0% |
| +25% | −6.25% | +6.25% | +25.0% | +37.5% |

**What to freeze for each candidate:**
- the quote date and session;
- the normalized EPS basis;
- the share count;
- enterprise obligations;
- optimistic, base and adverse assumptions.

**Validation design.** Compare the growth portfolio with:
- the equal-weight Mag Seven;
- a broad-equity benchmark;
- buy-and-hold at the same sleeve weights.

Report total return, maximum drawdown, recovery duration, upside capture, turnover, costs and missed rebounds.

**Valuation and monetization gate (v5.1 annex).** The first pass is in `monetization-valuation-gate-2026-10-29.md`. It gives a reverse DCF for each of the seven candidates, the monetization tests, and research tiers:
- **Provisionally qualified:** MSFT and TSM.
- **Conditional:** GOOGL (half weight) and NVDA (small).
- **Not yet:** AMZN, META and AVGO.

**Acceptance status:**
- The economic thesis is plausible.
- The company evidence is mixed but substantial.
- The 80/10/10 structure is a proposal.
- Individual valuations are incomplete.
- The exit rules are untested but now preregistered.

**Decision.** Keep a growth-first 2027 mandate. Select companies for sustainable per-share economics and entry valuation. Limit the shared AI risk, and judge protection by how it performs when it is late or wrong.

## 11 / Implementation corrections
**Meta financing.** The pricing release specifies:
- $25bn of common stock sold forward at $625, about 40m shares;
- $12.5bn of 6.50% mandatory convertible preferred, before any additional offering options.

How the financing works:
- The common-stock proceeds are expected at forward settlement, on or before 19 November 2027.
- Preferred dividends accrue at about $812.5m a year on the base issue.
- Conversion adds separate potential dilution, so the roughly 1.6% from the common leg is not the total.
- Class C conversion depends on specified conditions [14].

**The transition is not a constraint (v5.1).** The owner treats current holdings as changeable, so they are not used to set weights. Costs and taxes of any change still count when the time comes. No transition or sale is authorized by this paper.

**When the BTC gate is closed,** unallocated crypto capital goes to cash or short bills, not automatically into stocks or altcoins.

**Diversifying equity candidates:**
- equal-weight or ex-tech US;
- developed international;
- healthcare;
- staples;
- energy.

Energy may benefit from higher oil, but it is not a guaranteed Hormuz hedge.

**Single-issuer shock.** A single issuer falling 60% costs 6 portfolio points at 10% exposure, or 7.2 points at 12% including funds.

**Amendment 1 (v5.1), registered before any data was downloaded.** It settles the details the review found ambiguous; the original entry is unchanged:
- **Signal per sleeve:** QQQ drives the platform sleeve, SMH the supplier sleeve, and RSP and EFA the other-equities sleeve. Signals come from ETFs, never from individual stocks.
- **Prices:** total-return adjusted closes, with expense ratios already inside ETF prices. Cash earns the 3-month T-bill rate.
- **Holiday weeks:** a holiday-shortened week uses its last trading day.
- **Drawdown:** measured on daily marked portfolio value.
- **CAGR test:** applied to the full sample, and reported separately for development and holdout.
- **Rebalancing:** while a sleeve is at half, the other half sits in cash and other sleeves are not rebalanced into it.
- **Risk review:** the 10% drawdown review ends after 4 weeks, or sooner if the portfolio recovers to within 5% of its high.
- **Fixed criteria:** acceptance criteria cannot change after results are seen.

**Registry status (v5): registered, not yet tested.** The test is `equity-sleeve-40w-sma-half-exit-v1`, added to `docs/research/registry/tests_registry.jsonl` at 2026-10-29T08:59Z. No price history had been downloaded and no results had been seen [17].

What the registration fixes:
- **Benchmarks:** QQQ and SMH are primary; SPY, RSP and EFA are secondary. The single-stock basket is reported separately, labelled as hindsight-selected.
- **Signal:** 40-week SMA on completed weekly closes; two closes are needed to exit or re-enter; exit is to half.
- **Execution:** at the next session's open.
- **Variants:** exit to zero, and execution delayed by 1 or 2 weeks.
- **Costs:** 0.05%, 0.10% and 0.25% per side.
- **Splits:** development through 2012, validation 2013–2018, holdout from 2019. The holdout is computed only after the earlier results are written.

**Decision rule.** The rule is supported only if all three hold on both QQQ and SMH at 0.10% per side:
- maximum drawdown is at least 25% smaller in development and in holdout separately;
- the CAGR shortfall is no worse than 1.5 pp a year;
- the 1-week-delay variant also meets the drawdown condition.

**Disclosed bias.** The registrant already knows the shape of the 2000, 2008, 2020 and 2022 drawdowns, and the 40-week rule is famous partly because of them. The holdout carries the weight. A supported result still needs a separate owner decision before it becomes any live rule.
