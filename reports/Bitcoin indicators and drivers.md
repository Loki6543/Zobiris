# Daily trend filters beat Bitcoin's popular indicators

The technical signal with the best evidence for Bitcoin is the simplest one: **where price sits relative to a moving average on daily bars**. Next come **N-day-high breakouts** (Donchian-style) and time-series trend filters. RSI, MACD, SuperTrend and Bollinger Bands all have weaker or no rigorous support. Zobiris's own fee-included backtest on Kraken BTC daily bars (about 520 days ending October 2026, with 0.36% fees plus slippage per trade) agrees on the ranking. In that test **price above the 200-day SMA was the only rule that clearly beat buy-and-hold**: +12.6% with a −21.1% max drawdown, against −20.2% and −53.1% for buy-and-hold. SuperTrend (10,3) lost 11.4%, and both Bollinger variants lost 22–30%. The sample is small and covers mostly one falling market, so it confirms the direction of the evidence, not its size. Among non-technical drivers, almost none reliably predict BTC's direction over the next few days. ETF flows mostly follow price. Strategy's buying has stalled. Bank-adoption headlines are slow structural news. Wars and FOMC/CPI releases are **volatility events, not directional ones**. Liquidation spikes and on-chain valuation metrics work as regime and risk inputs rather than day-ahead calls. The honest goal for a daily forecast is therefore modest: directional accuracy of **about 51–55%** and a Brier skill score of a few percent over a climatology baseline. The homepage should be built around probabilities, volatility ranges and public scoring, not confident price targets.

## Moving averages and breakouts lead a short, weak list

The academic record ranks trend rules first, and the margin is narrow. Detzel and co-authors found that the **ratio of BTC price to its 5- to 100-day moving averages forecasts next-day returns both in and out of sample**. Trading on it gave economically significant alpha and Sharpe gains over buy-and-hold ([Financial Management 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/finmgt/v50y2021i1p107-137.html); [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/currency-trading/timing-bitcoin-with-smas/)). Padyšák and Vojtko showed that when BTC closes at a **10- to 50-day high, the next day's return is higher and less risky**, and shorter lookbacks worked best. Closes at a new low behave differently and look more like mean reversion ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/trend-following-and-mean-reversion-in-bitcoin/)). In a Quantpedia multi-timeframe example, a daily trend filter **cut max drawdown from −23.9% to −12.4% and lifted Sharpe from 0.33 to 0.80** ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/how-to-design-a-simple-multi-timeframe-trend-strategy-on-bitcoin/)). That points to where trend rules really help on BTC: **they reduce drawdowns, not beat bull markets**.

A sobering counterweight sits beside these results. Hudson and Urquhart tested about **15,000 rules** across moving-average, filter, support/resistance, channel-breakout and on-balance-volume classes, using data-snooping-robust tests. They found in-sample profits in every class, but **no out-of-sample predictability for Bitcoin**, even though some other coins showed it ([Annals of Operations Research 2021](https://hull-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/2639834)). Liu's study puts technical predictability of BTC at statistically significant but tiny, with **out-of-sample R² up to about 0.52%** ([Physica A](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/phsmap/v533y2019ics0378437119311380.html)). On that basis the top five, in order of evidence, are: price versus moving average, including the 200-day trend filter; N-day-high or Donchian breakout; time-series momentum or trend filters as drawdown control; MA crossovers; and OBV/volume. OBV was tested but never shown to be better than MA rules ([Hudson & Urquhart](https://hull-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/2639834)). RSI and MACD barely appear in rigorous BTC studies.

Zobiris's own backtest compares these head to head after fees. Over about 520 daily bars after a 200-day warmup, BTC buy-and-hold lost 20.2%, with a 53.1% max drawdown. Each trade was charged 0.36% for fees plus slippage.

| Rule (daily, Kraken BTC) | Total return | Sharpe | Max DD | Trades |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Price > 200D SMA | **+12.6%** | **0.47** | **−21.1%** | 9 |
| Price > 50D SMA | +1.2% | 0.16 | −29.2% | 35 |
| 20/50 SMA cross | +1.0% | 0.16 | −33.7% | 15 |
| RSI 14 > 50 | −10.7% | −0.17 | −33.9% | 72 |
| SuperTrend (10,3) | −11.4% | −0.23 | −37.4% | 15 |
| MACD > signal | −19.4% | −0.43 | −29.0% | 46 |
| Buy-and-hold | −20.2% | — | −53.1% | 1 |
| Bollinger mean-revert (buy < lower, exit > mid) | −21.9% | −0.54 | −39.9% | 18 |
| Donchian 20D breakout (exit 10D low) | −26.3% | −0.78 | −39.9% | 20 |
| Bollinger breakout (buy > upper, exit < mid) | −29.7% | −1.29 | −38.2% | 24 |

Three things stand out. First, the slow MA filters beat everything else **mainly by being out of the market during the decline**: every MA rule roughly halved buy-and-hold's drawdown. Second, the fast, high-turnover rules (RSI with 72 trades, MACD with 46) lost much of their edge to costs, as the cost-sensitivity literature predicts. Third, the 20-day Donchian breakout did poorly here even though the next-day breakout effect is well documented. Whipsaw in a choppy, falling market hurts breakout systems most, and one 520-day window cannot overturn a multi-cycle result. **The 200-day result rests on just nine trades.** One or two well-timed exits could produce that edge by chance, so the table is supporting evidence, not proof.

## SuperTrend and Bollinger Bands have the least evidence

No peer-reviewed or out-of-sample study of **SuperTrend** on Bitcoin turned up. The available evidence is TradingView scripts and blogs, and one author admits their version was never formally backtested ([TradingView](https://cn.tradingview.com/script/WvCVRGU1-SuperTrend)). Mechanically, SuperTrend is an ATR-band trailing stop: a trend follower with a volatility-scaled exit. It should behave like the MA and breakout family, but with a tighter exit that whipsaws more. In Zobiris's test it did worse than both the 50-day and 200-day filters (−11.4%, max drawdown −37.4%). It still beat buy-and-hold on drawdown, as any trend rule would in a bear market.

**Bollinger Bands** have one small journal study on BTC futures. It reports average holding-period returns above 20%, rising above 50% when the moving average is stretched from 20 to 60 days, and it does not say how costs were modelled ([Applied Economics Letters 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/apeclt/v30y2023i11p1437-1443.html)). That parameter sensitivity is itself a warning sign, and the improvement comes from making the bands behave more like a slow trend filter. Fading the bands works against BTC's documented momentum at new highs. Platform backtests that look spectacular are usually artifacts. One QuantConnect run shows a Sharpe of 45.7 with $0 fees, and a TradingView BB+RSI script was optimised for Sharpe on a single year ([QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/terminal/cache/embedded_backtest_6f9d52c7d250a4570ab127ed8b1b0541.html); [TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/script/i3Uc79fF/)). In Zobiris's backtest both Bollinger variants did worse than buy-and-hold on return. The breakout version had the **worst Sharpe of any rule tested (−1.29)**. The verdict is that both are fine as visual chart overlays, since Bollinger width is a good volatility gauge, but neither has earned a place in the forecast.

## Daily bars carry the signal; intraday charts are for timing

The evidence clusters on one timeframe. **Daily bars forecasting the next day to a few weeks** is where MA ratios and 10–50-day highs show predictive power ([Detzel et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/finmgt/v50y2021i1p107-137.html); [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/trend-following-and-mean-reversion-in-bitcoin/)). Intraday edges exist in-sample but fade. Corbet and co-authors found moving-average rules, especially variable-length ones, did best on 1-minute BTC data ([Finance Research Letters 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v31y2019icp32-37.html)). Yet Bakker's test of 3,312 rules on 5-minute data found profits **highly unstable and declining over time** ([Erasmus](https://thesis.eur.nl/pub/41546/Bakker.pdf)). A 1-minute range-breakout study found the edge weakened after 2017 and **did not beat buy-and-hold after costs** ([NCCU](https://thesis.lib.nccu.edu.tw/detail/5ee3a21ae846b1c833ff57e17695599b/)). The best hourly machine-learning models reach an **AUC of about 0.508** ([arXiv 2607.23370](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.23370)). No rigorous study of 4h, 8h or 12h bars was found at all.

| Chart timeframe | Evidence strength | Best use | Forecast horizon it serves | Default indicators |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1h | Weak; decays after costs | Execution timing, event reactions (FOMC/CPI first hour) | Hours | 20/50 EMA, volume, VWAP (visual only) |
| 4h | Untested; inferred from intraday decay | Swing entry timing inside the daily trend | 1–3 days | 20/50 EMA, ATR bands |
| 8h | Untested; aligns with funding intervals | Funding/positioning overlay | 1–3 days | Funding rate, OI change |
| 12h | Untested | Rarely needed; optional | 1–3 days | Same as 4h |
| **Daily (default)** | **Strongest: MA ratios, N-day highs** | **Primary forecast chart** | **1, 3, 7 days** | **50D and 200D SMA, 20D high/low channel, ATR(14), Bollinger width** |
| Weekly | Cycle-scale; few independent samples | Regime context | 30+ days | 50W/200W SMA, Mayer multiple, MVRV |

The practical mapping follows. A **next-day** forecast leans on daily price-to-MA ratios and closeness to the 10–20-day high. A **3–7 day** forecast uses daily bars with 10- to 50-day lookbacks. A **30-day** view uses the 200-day or weekly trend filter. Tuning should favour round, lightly tuned settings that sit on a plateau across nearby values, because a large rule search mainly inflates in-sample results ([Glasgow thesis](https://theses.gla.ac.uk/82986)).

## Flows, Saylor and the Fed move volatility more than direction

**Spot ETF flows** appear to follow price rather than lead it. The one Granger test found, on futures ETFs over 2021–2024, shows BTC prices predicting ETF prices and not the reverse ([vjol](https://vjol.info.vn/due/article/view/125157)). For spot ETFs, cointegration between net assets and price holds only at the 10% level, and the authors describe flows as both driving and chasing returns ([Ledger](https://ledger.pitt.edu/ojs/ledger/article/view/393)). Daily figures are also unreliable on the day itself. Trackers disagreed over which day a +$102.7M October 2026 print belonged to ([TFTC](https://www.tftc.io/bitcoin-etf-flows/october-2026)). Recent data shows a recovery, though not a lead signal. Flows ran about **−$2.91B year-to-date by late August 2026**, followed by **+$2.65B in September**, and month-to-date October inflows reached **+$321.4M by October 6, with BTC near $86k** ([IDNFinancials](https://www.idnfinancials.com/digital-asset/69849/bitcoin-etfs-absorb-us134-4-million-in-early-october); [KuCoin/CoinDesk](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/bitcoin-etfs-see-118-8m-net-inflow-on-october-6-2026)). BlackRock's IBIT dominates the inflows, but no study isolates its effect from the sector's. A 5-day rolling sum works as a confirmation input, lagged one day.

**Strategy (MicroStrategy)** has gone from steady bid to potential overhang. On August 17, 2026 it held **840,447 BTC at a $75,385 average cost**, bought nothing that week, and traded at an **enterprise mNAV of 1.04**, down from a 3.29 peak in November 2024 ([The Block](https://theblock.co/news/business/2026-08-17-michael-saylor-strategy-btc-411942)). It made its first-ever sale (32 BTC) in late May 2026 and has turned to preferred stock for funding. Management reportedly cited an mNAV of about **1.22x** as the level below which selling BTC to pay dividends beats issuing equity ([BitMEX](https://www.bitmex.com/es/blog/strategy-earning-recap); [KuCoin](https://kucoin.com/blog/tr-strategy-death-spiral-debunked-why-mstr-s-crypto-flywheel-paused-as-strc-preferred-stock-hits-75-amid-bitcoin-slump?x=tr_TR)). With BTC only about 14% above Strategy's cost basis, the item to monitor is **mNAV, not the weekly 8-K buy headline**. No study measures the price impact of those buys. **Bank adoption** keeps advancing: JPMorgan client purchases and IBIT-linked notes, Morgan Stanley's planned E*Trade spot trading, and Bank of America's 1–4% allocation guidance ([ETF.com](https://www.etf.com/sections/news/jpmorgan-vanguard-bitcoin-etf-crypto-trading); [Sherlock](https://sherlock.xyz/post/institutional-crypto-adoption-in-2026-whos-actually-moving-in)). No event study shows any of these moving price over days, so they get zero forecast weight.

**Macro** sets the backdrop. BTC behaves like a high-beta, liquidity-sensitive tech asset. Its correlation with the Nasdaq-100 ran **+0.35 to +0.6 through 2025 and early 2026**, and its dollar correlation fell from about −0.4 to near zero. In February 2026, a rise in the 10-year yield from 4.0% to 4.3% alongside a firmer dollar coincided with a BTC sell-off ([Interactive Brokers/CME](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/?p=238789)). Short correlation windows are unreliable: the 25-day BTC–Nasdaq correlation was about zero on May 11, 2026 ([Sharpe.ai](https://www.sharpe.ai/correlation/bitcoin-vs-nasdaq)). Global M2 reportedly leads BTC by about **8–10 weeks**, with r=0.94 over 2013–2024. That is an in-sample industry fit, and the rate of change matters more than the level ([Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/markets/global-liquidity-fuels-bitcoin-price); [LookIntoBitcoin](https://www.lookintobitcoin.com/bitcoin-research/the-global-liquidity-influence-on-bitcoin/)). Morgan Stanley's figure that monthly returns ran about 3 points higher when M2 accelerated supports a small weight, not a large one ([Newhedge](https://newhedge.io/bitcoin/bitcoin-vs-m2-10-week-lead)). **Fed and CPI** days are surprise-driven. The NY Fed found BTC largely unaffected by macro surprises over 2017–2022, with CPI the only release significant at 5% ([NY Fed SR1052](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr1052.pdf?sc_lang=en)). Mean absolute first-hour returns roughly double on FOMC days ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v101y2026ics1544612326006021.html)), and the first move tends to **reverse within about four hours** ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4478184)). In-line CPI in September 2025 left BTC flat, while a soft print sent it up ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/370335/bitcoin-holds-114k-markets-september-rate-cuts-cpi-expectations); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bitcoin-spikes-112k-soft-us-cpi-data-sp-500-hits-record-high)).

**Geopolitical shocks** look the same in the data: a sharp drop with a wide range, then recovery. When the US and Israel struck Iran on February 28, 2026, BTC fell **1.8% on the day with a 7.4% intraday range**. After the 2022 Ukraine invasion it was up 11% at seven days, before Fed tightening drove it down 44% over 180 days. Since 2020, the average 90-day return after major shocks is about +11%, with wide dispersion ([analysis](https://san-1510.aid.brainsum.com/news/analysis/bitcoin-wartime-tale-two-shocks); [follow-up](https://san-1510.aid.brainsum.com/news/analysis/bitcoin-and-geopolitical-shocks-revisiting-data)). That source looks like a mirror site, so the figures are indicative. The rule for the homepage is to **widen the forecast range on a shock headline without flipping the directional call** unless yields and the dollar confirm.

## Liquidations and on-chain data set the regime, not tomorrow's close

No peer-reviewed study gives 1–7 day forward returns after long- or short-liquidation spikes. Even Glassnode-cited coverage notes that **long-liquidation dominance peaks do not reliably mark bottoms**. One example: dominance hit 69% in January 2022 before further losses ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/markets/bitcoin-batters-longs-as-liquidations-copy-may-2021-run-to-30k); [Glassnode](https://studio.glassnode.com/charts/derivatives.FuturesLiquidatedVolumeLongRelative?a=BTC)). A study of seven BTC-perpetual cascades from 2022 to 2025, including the roughly $19B event on October 10, 2025, found **no early-warning variable that held across all events** ([arXiv 2607.27070](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.27070)). What does persist is fragility afterwards. On Hyperliquid after October 10, order-book depth stayed at **66–79% of pre-event levels for 23 days** ([JFDA](https://journals.lincoln.ac.nz/index.php/JFDA/article/view/1395)). Glassnode also warns that funding and liquidation series lag ([Glassnode](https://insights.glassnode.com/leverage-position-openings-and-closures)). Liquidation imbalance therefore belongs in the **volatility forecast**, along with a testable hypothesis to score live: mean reversion after extreme long flushes when funding turns negative and open interest drops, and continuation after short squeezes while open interest rebuilds.

On-chain valuation metrics work on a cycle scale. **MVRV-Z and NUPL beat buy-and-hold for cycle timing over 2013–2025**, but that rests on three cycles and a fixed sample ([Univ. of Vaasa](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/items/48a4f9df-03ca-4b96-9072-07b462b14dfa/full)). Popular thresholds such as MVRV above 3.5 or below 1 come from blogs. The on-chain signal with the best support at the scale of days is **exchange reserves**: rising reserves line up with lower current and future returns and higher volatility ([Quantpedia on Hoang & Baur](https://quantpedia.com/bitcoin-returns-and-volatility-predicted-by-bitcoin-exchange-reserves/)). That signal is noisy because of wallet labelling and OTC flows ([BIT](https://www.bit.com/insights/knowledge-hub/exchange-net-flow)). The earlier caveat still applies: CryptoQuant's founder concedes that on-chain data cannot see ETF and OTC buying ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bitcoin-whales-hint-80k-market-rebound-binance-inflows-cool)).

Accuracy expectations need to be set low, and stated publicly. A 2021 study fell from about 4% misclassification in-sample to **41% out-of-sample** ([MASA](https://content.iospress.com/articles/model-assisted-statistics-and-applications/mas210530)). A credible daily random forest reached **58.18%** on a single 10% hold-out ([CRORR](https://ojs.srce.hr/crorr/article/view/27587)). Claims of 99% accuracy indicate leakage ([Gazi Univ.](https://avesis.gazi.edu.tr/yayin/dc987843-0331-45fa-966d-1336f40ff368/bitcoin-price-direction-prediction-using-machine-learning-on-a-very-small-dataset)). With about 250 scored days, the 95% confidence band is roughly ±6 points, so a model showing 55% cannot yet be told apart from a coin flip.

## Homepage specification: chart, forecast, sources and scoring

**Chart.** Open on the **daily** timeframe. Offer **weekly** for regime context and **4h** for timing, with **1h** kept for event days. The evidence does not support 8h or 12h as main views. Show 8h only as the funding-rate overlay, and drop 12h or keep it as a hidden option. Default daily overlays are the 50D and 200D SMA, a 20-day high/low channel, and ATR(14) in a lower pane. SuperTrend, Bollinger Bands, RSI and MACD can be toggled on, each labelled "**not used in forecast — no fee-adjusted edge found**." A regime strip under the chart shows trend state (above or below the 200D SMA), Mayer multiple, MVRV and Strategy mNAV.

**Daily forecast.** Publish each day at 00:05 UTC, using only data available by 00:00 UTC. The output is **P(up) for 1, 3 and 7 days** plus a **68% price range** from ATR-scaled volatility. Starting from climatology (the trailing 365-day share of up periods), add log-odds contributions with the starting weights below. These weights are priors to be refit, not estimates.

| Input | Data / transform | Start weight (share of signal budget) | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trend: price / 200D SMA | z-scored, capped | 30% | Direction |
| Short trend: price / 20D SMA and distance to 20D high | z-scored | 20% | Direction (1–3d) |
| ETF flows | 5-day sum, T+1 lag, z-score | 10% | Confirmation |
| Macro: QQQ 5d return, DXY and DFII10 5d change | z-scores, signs per evidence | 15% | Direction |
| Liquidity: net liquidity (WALCL−TGA−RRP) and M2 YoY lagged 10 weeks | weekly, rate of change | 5% | Slow prior |
| Positioning: funding, OI change, long/short liquidation z-score | Binance public | 10% | Contrarian at extremes; volatility |
| On-chain: exchange netflow, MVRV regime | Coin Metrics | 5% | Regime / volatility |
| Strategy mNAV < 1.2 flag | strategy.com / EDGAR | 5% | Downside tail |
| Event flags: FOMC, CPI, shock headline | calendars | 0% direction | Widen range ×1.3–1.5 |

The total signal shift is capped so that **no forecast exceeds P(up)=0.65** until the live record supports more confidence. Bank-adoption headlines get no weight.

**Free data sources.** Use the Kraken public OHLC API for prices, with Yahoo's chart endpoint for BTC-USD, QQQ, DX-Y.NYB, IBIT and MSTR. Pull macro series from FRED's keyless `fredgraph.csv` (WALCL, WTREGEN, RRPONTSYD, M2SL, DFII10, DTWEXBGS, DFF, CPIAUCSL). ETF flows come from Farside (T+1, scraped gently), with CoinShares as a weekly cross-check. Strategy filings come from SEC EDGAR at `data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK0001050446.json`, which requires a User-Agent header. For positioning, use Binance's `!forceOrder@arr` WebSocket and the funding and OI REST endpoints ([Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/websocket-market-streams/All-Market-Liquidation-Order-Streams)). **Start archiving these now**, because history is capped and free liquidation history does not exist; Coinglass's API starts at about $29 a month ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/d/data-providers/coinglass/)). On-chain data comes from the Coin Metrics Community API, keyless at 10 requests per 6 seconds ([Coin Metrics](https://docs.coinmetrics.io/packages/coin-metrics-community-data)), plus mempool.space. Event dates come from the federalreserve.gov FOMC calendar and the bls.gov CPI schedule. The FRED, Yahoo and blockchain endpoints were not live-tested in this research.

**Scoring and adaptation.** Store every forecast with a timestamp before the outcome is known. Score it against **three baselines**: climatology, "always up", and a 50% random walk. Publish the following on the page:

- directional accuracy with a binomial 95% confidence interval;
- **Brier score and Brier skill score against climatology**, plus log loss;
- a **reliability diagram** with expected calibration error, using deciles of P(up);
- hit rate for the price ranges, where the 68% range should contain the outcome about 68% of the time;
- a Diebold-Mariano test against the random walk for magnitude.

Refit the weights **monthly** with an expanding-window logistic regression and a purge gap equal to the horizon (7 days for the 7-day target). Apply L2 shrinkage toward the priors above. Every 90 days, drop any input whose out-of-sample contribution to Brier skill has not been positive. Recalibrate probabilities with isotonic or Platt scaling once at least 200 scored days exist. Until then, show a banner stating that the sample is too small to tell skill from luck. Run the liquidation mean-reversion and continuation hypotheses as shadow forecasts that are scored but carry no weight, and promote them only if they earn it.

## Conclusion

The research supports a homepage that predicts less than most crypto sites do, and gets more credit for it. The edge in Bitcoin technicals is mostly the ability to **stay out of large drawdowns**, which slow daily trend filters capture. The fashionable indicators mostly add trades and costs. Zobiris's backtest shows this clearly: the 200-day filter turned a 53% drawdown into 21% with nine trades, while SuperTrend and Bollinger lost money. The non-technical drivers mostly change **how far** BTC is likely to move, not **which way**. ETF flows, Fed days, wars and liquidation cascades all widen the distribution more than they shift it. The useful product is therefore a calibrated probability with an honest range, beside a public scorecard against climatology. This approach can be trusted because it will show when it has no edge.

The biggest open questions are ones only Zobiris's own live data can settle. Does liquidation imbalance carry next-day information once it is normalised by open interest? Does a Strategy mNAV below 1.2 come before measurable selling pressure? Does the 200-day filter's edge survive a bull market, where it will lag? Logging the free Binance, Coin Metrics and EDGAR feeds from day one turns the homepage into the backtest that the literature has so far failed to provide.
